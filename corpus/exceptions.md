## Super Rod — the 2000 printing is played with the 2023 text

Play ANY card named Super Rod as: shuffle **up to 3** in any combination of Pokémon
and Basic Energy cards from your discard pile into your deck.

This includes the Neo Genesis (2000) printing, whose printed text is a completely
different effect — flip a coin, heads puts an Evolution card from your discard pile
into your hand, tails a Basic Pokémon. None of that applies. A card is identified by
its name, so the newest printing's text replaces the text on every earlier one, no
matter how far apart they read.

"Up to" means the player may choose 1, 2, or 3, not that they must take 3.

If a player shows you the 2000 card and argues the words on it, the words on it are
not the card. Ask them to look up the current text.

Overrides: "a card does what it says".
Source: printed texts of neo1-103, bw3-95 and sv02-188 read from TCGdex 2026-09-05;
the governing-text rule from the TPCi errata list. NEEDS REVIEW — confirm against

## Alternate attack costs cannot be modified

Where a card sets an attack cost outright — "this attack costs [F]", rather than
reducing the printed cost — that is an alternate cost. Nothing else increases or
decreases it. Cost-reduction effects do not apply on top.

Not derivable: one reading says reductions apply to whatever the cost currently is,
the other says a set cost replaces the cost and is not open to modification. Both
are defensible from the rulebook. TPCi settled on the second.

Overrides: "attack cost reductions apply to the current cost".
Source: TPCi Rules Team. NEEDS REVIEW.
